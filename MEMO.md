# Memo to Ritu - returns

**The decision.** Do not hold risky orders. Call the customer before dispatch instead. A hold does nothing to prevent a return on its own, and about 12% of held customers cancel. A confirmation call prevented about 35% of returns in your spring pilot.

**The number.** 95% accuracy is not possible, and it is the wrong goal: only 11.5% of orders come back, so "nothing returns" is already 88.5% right. The model is for ranking. In a test on April-June 2026 orders it put the riskiest 10% at about 37% chance of return, against 12% overall. It finds about 64% of returns in the riskiest 30% of orders, but 3 in 4 of those calls go to customers who would have kept the item.

**The rupees** (return Rs 1,150, call Rs 45). At about 700 orders a month: call 210 customers = Rs 9,450; about 18 returns prevented = about Rs 20,700; **net about Rs 11,000 a month, about Rs 1.3 lakh a year.** Calling everyone nets almost nothing. If calls prevent only half as many returns as in the pilot, the net falls to about Rs 900 a month. The model itself costs nothing per order.

**Next week.**
1. Decide how Shield members are treated. The model flags them about twice as often; a pointless call to them is the costliest mistake.
2. Run a 3-4 week test: call a random half of the flagged orders, leave the other half alone, compare returns. This gives the real saving.
3. Ask Tanmay to confirm the October order-value fix, and to supply order-date versions of customer return counts and Shield status.
4. Tell the board the target is rupees saved, not 95% accuracy.
